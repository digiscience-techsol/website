Cloud cost governance for Indian enterprises

Cloud bills in Indian organisations usually pass through finance, engineering and often a partner or reseller before anyone can approve a cut. DigiScience, an India-based team, writes down who decides, under which constraints, and what stays on hold. Fixed scope, no savings-share.

Where Indian cloud estates get stuck

  • Split ownership: the budget sits with finance or a business unit, the resources with an engineering team, and the invoice with a partner. Nobody can approve a change alone.
  • INR budgets, foreign-currency or partner-billed spend: annual plans are set in rupees on an April to March fiscal year, while cloud usage is variable. Month-end variance needs a named owner, not a spreadsheet chase.
  • Region and data limits: workloads kept in India regions (for example AWS Mumbai or Hyderabad, Azure Central India, GCP Mumbai or Delhi) may not be freely moved or consolidated to cut cost.
  • Regulated or personal data: banks, insurers and healthcare teams, and anyone handling personal data under India’s DPDP Act, often need compliance sign-off before storage, logging or backup changes. Cost work has to respect that.
  • Commitments bought centrally: reserved capacity or savings plans purchased by one team but consumed by several, with no agreed way to share the benefit or the risk.

This is general context, not legal or tax advice. Your compliance and finance teams make those calls; we make them visible before any spend decision.

What the governance package covers

  • Decision-rights table: who can approve, who must be consulted, and who is informed, for each spend category
  • Ownership map: owned vs shared vs unclear spend, with named decision owners where known
  • Constraint register: region, data and compliance limits that rule out certain cuts
  • Act / investigate / hold list with blast-radius notes for the first safe changes
  • A 30/60/90 written next-step plan finance and engineering can both use

Questions we ask in the first call

  1. Who signs off a cloud budget change today, and how long does that take?
  2. Which accounts or subscriptions are billed through a partner, and which directly?
  3. Which workloads must stay in India regions or need compliance review before changes?
  4. Which commitments or discounts are shared across teams, and who owns the utilisation?
