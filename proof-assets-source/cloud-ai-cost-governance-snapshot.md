# Cloud + AI Cost Governance Snapshot

DigiScience Techsol | Planning brief | 21 September 2026

Start with one defined cost question. Separate higher demand from a higher cost per useful result before changing resources. This brief describes an assessment approach, not measured savings or completed customer work.

## Build a comparable baseline
Use the same billing scope, currency and period length. Separate credits, taxes and one-off charges. Group spending by workload, environment and owner; document how shared services are allocated. Match usage to accepted business outcomes and retain failed or retried requests as visible costs.

## Check the whole workflow
Review idle capacity, storage growth, data transfer, logging and model usage where relevant. AI workflows may also incur retrieval, embeddings, indexing and human-review costs. Confirm a resource's purpose before proposing removal. Budget alerts are not automatically spending caps.

## Work one transparent example
Fictional example: INR 12,000 allocated workflow cost divided by 3,000 accepted results equals INR 4 per accepted result. Include failed attempts in the agreed cost boundary. Define acceptance first, then compare quality, latency, rejection rate and workload mix alongside cost. These numbers are invented arithmetic, not provider prices or a savings claim.

## Make the next decision explicit
Record the cost question, workload owner, billing period, activity count, largest unexplained driver and operational constraints. Decide whether the next step is clearer ownership, a usage policy or a separately tested technical change. For any implementation, define the owner, quality checks, comparison period and rollback condition first.
