# First Discovery Call Readiness Pack

Date: 2026-05-26
Status: Internal discovery readiness pack. Do not publish.

Purpose: prepare DigiScience to book and conduct the first qualified discovery call without over-scoping, over-claiming, or sending unapproved outreach.

## Primary Offer To Sell First

AI Readiness Assessment.

## Recommended First Offer

AI Readiness Assessment focused on one workflow, one business owner, and one practical go/no-go decision for a possible 45-day pilot.

## Target Industries

- Legal
- BFSI/Insurance
- Manufacturing

## Discovery Call Agenda

1. Confirm the customer context, role, and business priority.
2. Identify one workflow with visible manual effort, delay, cost, risk, or quality issues.
3. Understand current process, data/document sources, systems, and owners.
4. Assess whether AI support is feasible, useful, and safe.
5. Discuss security, confidentiality, governance, human review, and compliance constraints.
6. Agree whether the next step should be an AI Readiness Assessment, a 45-day pilot scoping discussion, a governance review, or no action.
7. Confirm next owner, timeline, and required inputs.

## Qualification Questions

- Which workflow is causing the most visible time loss, cost leakage, review delay, or operational risk?
- Who owns the workflow today?
- How often does the workflow happen?
- What documents, data, tickets, claims, records, or equipment signals are involved?
- What is the current manual effort or cycle time?
- What errors, delays, escalations, or rework happen today?
- What would a useful improvement look like in business terms?
- Is there an internal sponsor who can review outputs and make a go/no-go decision?
- Is the team exploring AI now, or only researching future options?
- Is there budget or approval path for a small assessment or pilot if the fit is clear?

## Customer Inputs Needed

- One candidate workflow.
- Current process description.
- Approximate volume, frequency, or workload.
- Current systems or repositories involved.
- Known constraints around data, privacy, compliance, and access.
- Business owner and reviewer names or roles.
- Success criteria for a readiness assessment or pilot.
- Decision timeline and approval path.

## Security / Governance Questions

- What data or documents are sensitive, confidential, regulated, or client/customer-owned?
- Can the first assessment use sample, anonymized, synthetic, or approved non-sensitive data?
- Who must approve access to documents, data, systems, or cloud resources?
- What human review is required before any AI-supported output is used?
- Are audit trails, source citations, access logs, or approval records required?
- Are there specific cloud, data residency, security, or vendor onboarding requirements?
- What risks would make the customer reject an AI pilot?
- What compliance, legal, infosec, procurement, or risk teams must be involved?

## Expected Outcome Of The Call

The call should produce one of four outcomes:

- Proceed to AI Readiness Assessment proposal.
- Continue scoping because the workflow is promising but inputs are incomplete.
- Redirect to Responsible AI Governance Review if risk and control questions are the main issue.
- Hold/nurture if there is no clear workflow, owner, urgency, or approval path.

## Next-Step Options After Call

- Send a paid AI Readiness Assessment proposal.
- Schedule a technical/security scoping call.
- Request a sample workflow description and non-sensitive sample artifacts.
- Prepare a 45-day pilot outline if workflow fit, data readiness, and sponsor commitment are strong.
- Send a short recap and hold until the customer identifies an owner or use case.

## Proposal Trigger Criteria

Send an AI Readiness Assessment proposal only if the discovery call confirms:

- One named or role-defined workflow owner.
- One specific workflow.
- Clear business pain or operational risk.
- Available process/data/document inputs.
- Acceptable security and confidentiality path.
- Human reviewer availability.
- Decision-maker or sponsor interest.
- Agreement that a small paid assessment is a reasonable next step.

Do not send a proposal if the customer only wants generic AI education, has no workflow owner, cannot discuss data/process constraints, or has no path to a paid next step.
