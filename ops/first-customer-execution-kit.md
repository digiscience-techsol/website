# First Customer Execution Kit

Date: 2026-05-25
Status: Internal sales execution kit. Do not publish.

## 1. Target Account Shortlist Criteria

Shortlist accounts that have:

- A visible AI, automation, digital transformation, operations, risk, legal, supply chain, healthcare, HR, or customer experience priority.
- A workflow with measurable delay, manual effort, risk, compliance burden, rework, customer escalation, or cost leakage.
- A buyer who can sponsor a discovery conversation.
- Data that can be assessed safely using sample, redacted, anonymized, synthetic, or controlled export paths.
- A realistic first step: AI Readiness Assessment, Responsible AI Governance Review, Secure AI Cloud Platform, or a 45-day pilot.
- A credible path to a paid pilot within 30-60 days.
- No expectation that AI will make final regulated, clinical, legal, hiring, or safety decisions without human approval.

Avoid accounts where:

- The request is broad transformation with no workflow owner.
- The buyer wants free consulting with no next-step intent.
- Data access is impossible.
- Governance owners are absent for sensitive workflows.
- Success cannot be measured.

## 2. Best-Fit Buyer Profiles

- Founder / CEO: good for smaller firms where speed and ROI matter.
- COO / Operations Head: good for workflow delay, productivity, service quality, and cost leakage.
- CIO / CTO / Digital Head: good for secure AI platform, integration, and AI readiness.
- Compliance / Risk / Privacy Head: good for responsible AI governance and regulated workflows.
- Legal Head / General Counsel: good for document intelligence and compliance evidence.
- Plant / Manufacturing / Quality / Maintenance Head: good for predictive maintenance, OEE, inspection, and bottlenecks.
- Healthcare Operations / Digital Health Head: good for intake, documentation, and workflow support.
- Claims / Underwriting / Fraud Head: good for BFSI decision support and auditability.
- Talent Acquisition / HR Operations Head: good for recruiter workflow support with fairness controls.

## 5. Discovery Call Prep

Before the call:

- Identify target industry and likely workflow.
- Review website, leadership, services, recent news, hiring signals, and digital/AI language.
- Identify likely business pain in plain language.
- Prepare one workflow hypothesis.
- Prepare one measurable outcome hypothesis.
- Prepare safe data assumptions.
- Prepare governance risk notes.
- Choose one likely first offer.

Call goal:

- Confirm whether a real workflow pain exists.
- Confirm owner, data path, governance constraints, and measurable outcome.
- Recommend one next step.

## 6. Customer Qualification Checklist

Qualify:

- Business problem is specific.
- Workflow owner exists.
- Decision owner exists.
- Baseline metric exists or can be captured.
- Safe sample/data path exists.
- Human reviewer exists.
- Governance/security constraints are known.
- Timeline and budget signal exist.
- Buyer accepts a bounded first step.

Lead category:

- Hot: owner, problem, data path, metric, urgency, and sponsor are clear.
- Warm: problem exists but data, owner, metric, or approval path needs clarification.
- Nurture: interest exists but urgency, owner, or readiness is weak.
- Disqualify: no measurable problem, no owner, unsafe expectations, or no data path.

## 7. Proposal Response Workflow

1. Write a short problem summary in the customer's language.
2. Confirm workflow and owner.
3. Confirm proposed offer.
4. Define scope boundaries.
5. Define required data/access.
6. Define governance controls.
7. Define success metric.
8. Define timeline and review cadence.
9. Define deliverables.
10. Define assumptions and exclusions.
11. Send proposal only after internal review and founder approval.

## 8. Follow-Up Cadence

- Same day: send recap and next-step proposal path.
- Day 2: clarify missing data, owner, or success metric.
- Day 5: send concise next-step reminder.
- Day 10: send final useful follow-up with a smaller entry option.
- After Day 10: move to nurture unless buyer re-engages.

Do not spam. Do not send outreach without approval. LinkedIn remains `AUTH PENDING`.

## 9. Internal Operating Rhythm

Daily:

- Review new leads and inbound replies.
- Classify leads as Hot, Warm, Nurture, or Disqualified.
- Draft next-step responses for founder approval.

Weekly:

- Review target account shortlist.
- Review discovery calls and proposals.
- Review industry priority scorecard.
- Update blockers and next actions.

After each discovery call:

- Update call notes.
- Update recommended offer.
- Prepare proposal response draft.
- Track follow-up date.

## 10. Go / No-Go Rules

Go when:

- Workflow pain is clear.
- Buyer owns or influences the outcome.
- Data path is safe.
- Human approval path exists.
- Success metric is measurable.
- First step can be delivered in a bounded scope.

No-go when:

- Buyer expects autonomous high-risk decisions.
- No owner exists.
- No safe data path exists.
- No reviewer exists.
- Governance risk is ignored.
- The ask is broad, unpaid, or immeasurable.

Revise when:

- The use case is valuable but too broad.
- Real data is blocked but sample/synthetic data can validate workflow.
- Governance review must happen before pilot.
- Platform readiness must be established before use-case delivery.
