# Industry Discovery: Retail AI

Date: 2026-05-25
Status: Internal industry discovery material. Do not publish.

## Target Buyers

- Retail Operations Head
- Store Operations Head
- E-commerce Head
- Merchandising Head
- Inventory / Supply Chain Head
- Customer Experience Head
- CIO / CTO
- Digital Transformation Head

## Business Problems

- Store operations depend on manual checks and inconsistent execution.
- Stock-outs and overstock reduce revenue and margin.
- Demand signals are spread across POS, e-commerce, promotions, inventory, and external events.
- Customer support teams repeat answers across order status, returns, product, and policy questions.
- Inventory visibility across store, warehouse, and online channels is incomplete.
- Personalization requires privacy and governance controls.
- Campaign effectiveness analysis is slow.
- Returns create cost, workload, and customer friction.

## Discovery Questions

- Which retail workflow has the biggest revenue, margin, or customer experience impact?
- Is the priority stock-outs, demand signals, support workload, returns, campaign effectiveness, store operations, or personalization governance?
- Which channel should be assessed first: store, e-commerce, marketplace, call center, or omnichannel?
- What is the baseline: stock-out rate, lost sales, return rate, support volume, campaign conversion, inventory accuracy, or store task completion?
- Who owns the metric?
- What workflow support would be useful in 45 days?

## Data Readiness Questions

- What POS, order, product, inventory, customer support, campaign, returns, and store operations data exists?
- Are product IDs, SKUs, store IDs, and customer/order IDs consistent?
- Can sample or anonymized data be used first?
- Are policies, product descriptions, FAQs, and return rules approved for knowledge retrieval?
- Who approves data access and retention?

## Retail Workflow Questions

- How are stock-outs detected today?
- How are replenishment and transfer decisions made?
- How are customer support issues classified?
- How are returns reviewed?
- How are campaigns measured?
- Which tasks require human approval?
- What should AI never decide or communicate automatically?

## Privacy / Governance Questions

- Does data include personal data, payment-related data, loyalty data, or sensitive customer behavior?
- Is personalization allowed under current privacy rules?
- Are customer-facing recommendations governed?
- Is source citation needed for support answers?
- What audit trail is required for AI-assisted decisions?

## Success Metrics

- Reduce stock-out detection time.
- Improve inventory risk visibility.
- Reduce customer support handling time.
- Improve return reason classification.
- Improve campaign insight turnaround.
- Increase approved knowledge answer usefulness.
- Maintain privacy and human approval controls.

## Red Flags

- Customer expects unmanaged personalization without privacy controls.
- Product, SKU, order, or inventory IDs are inconsistent.
- No retail workflow owner is available.
- No approved support knowledge base exists.
- Customer wants AI to send customer messages without review.
- Success metric is undefined.

## Recommended First Offer Logic

Recommend AI Readiness Assessment when data, workflow priority, or governance is unclear.

Recommend retail workflow pilot when one bounded workflow, sample data, reviewer, and success metric exist.

Recommend recommendation/customer support pilot when approved product/support content exists and human approval controls are agreed.

Recommend Responsible AI Governance Review when personalization, customer data, or customer-facing AI risk is the blocker.
