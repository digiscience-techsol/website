Savings-share FinOps vs a fixed-fee diagnostic: which fits your cloud estate?

Considering a ProsperOps-style savings-share vendor? Here is when it fits, when it does not, and what to ask first. Written by a fixed-fee firm, so read the “when it fits” sections as carefully as ours.

The short version

Savings-share FinOps vendors (commitment-automation tools such as ProsperOps are the best-known example for AWS) are typically paid out of the savings they generate. A fixed-scope diagnostic is paid for a defined written deliverable. They solve different problems, and for many teams the honest answer is “diagnostic first, automation later”.

Five questions to ask any savings-share vendor

  1. How is the savings baseline calculated, and can we see it before signing?
  2. What happens to the fee if our usage drops or we migrate workloads?
  3. Which commitments are you managing, and what is our exposure if you stop?
  4. Does the fee apply to savings we would have made ourselves (tagging clean-up, rightsizing)?
  5. What do you leave untouched: shared platform cost, data transfer, AI/GPU, Kubernetes?

When savings-share is a good fit

  • Usage is stable, ownership is already clear, and commitment coverage is the obvious gap.
  • You prefer not to pay anything unless savings appear.
  • You do not have in-house capacity to manage Savings Plans / RIs.

When a fixed-fee diagnostic is the better first step

  • Nobody can say who owns shared EKS/AKS, logging, data transfer or AI spend.
  • You want a written act / investigate / hold register, not an automated purchase.
  • You want a 30/60/90-day plan your own team executes, with no percentage of savings taken.

Many teams run the diagnostic first, then add commitment automation once ownership is settled. We do not resell any vendor and do not take savings-share.

FAQ

Does DigiScience take a percentage of cloud savings?

No. DigiScience uses fixed-scope assessments and delivery packages. We do not use savings-share pricing.

Is savings-share pricing bad?

No. It can be a good fit when usage is stable and ownership is clear. The risks are an opaque savings baseline and gaps it does not address, such as unowned shared platform cost.

Can we use a diagnostic and a commitment-automation vendor together?

Yes. A common order is the ownership diagnostic first, then commitment automation on the spend you have confirmed is owned and stable.

Do you guarantee savings?

No. Recommendations are classified act, investigate or hold so thin evidence does not become a forced cut.
