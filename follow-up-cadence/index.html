Follow-up cadence

Follow up without becoming spammy

Use this cadence to stay useful, permission-based, and specific. Stop when there is no signal or the buyer declines.

Use message bankTrack follow-ups

Five-touch sequence

DayLinkedIn versionEmail versionWarm contact version
Day 0Send connection request tied to buyer role and one workflow.Send first email with one pain hypothesis and low-friction CTA.Send short note only if relationship allows it.
Day 2Short follow-up: ask about the business problem and workflow owner.Reply with one question: which workflow is most painful today?Ask if the topic is relevant now or later.
Day 5Share AI readiness angle: value, data, cloud, governance, pilot feasibility.Offer to share readiness scorecard structure.Send one-line readiness angle and ask permission.
Day 9Share relevant industry use case, such as legal document intelligence or predictive maintenance readiness.Send industry-specific use case note and ask whether the workflow exists.Share one industry example as a generic blueprint, not a case study.
Day 14Breakup note: close the loop unless AI readiness or governed pilots become a priority.Permission-based close: should I close the loop for now?Close gently and offer future help if timing changes.

Stop rules

  • Stop immediately if the buyer asks not to be contacted.
  • Stop after Day 14 if there is no signal.
  • Do not send more than one follow-up per channel in a short window.
  • Do not keep pushing if there is no workflow, no owner, or no timeline.

Move rules

  • Move to Nurture: interested but no timing, budget, or owner.
  • Mark as Qualified: buyer has workflow pain, sponsor, timeline, and data/system context.
  • Schedule Discovery: buyer agrees to discuss one workflow and expected outcome.
  • Proposal Needed: discovery confirms offer fit and commercial path.
